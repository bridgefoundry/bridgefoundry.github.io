Bridge Foundry's GoBridge has been proud to support GopherCon Africa.
The organizers recently sent us their report on this year's conference.

## The conference by the numbers

- **181 unique attendees** across three days, with an average of 146 people
  each day.
- **Go communities from eight countries:** Nigeria, Kenya, Rwanda, Uganda,
  South Sudan, Brazil, Portugal and the UK.
- **33 women attended**, more than double the number from previous years.
- **15 members of the Uganda Go community** received sponsorships to attend.

Half of all attendees are early in their Go journey. Among those who answered,
that's nearly three out of four. Tech conferences can feel like they're only
for experts, which keeps many people from ever walking through the door. At
GopherCon Africa, newcomers aren't on the sidelines. They're the majority, learning
alongside experienced developers who can mentor them. Making beginners feel
like they belong has been at the heart of Bridge Foundry since our first
workshop.

## How this furthers Bridge Foundry's mission

<img class="img-wrap-right" src="{{ site.github.url }}/assets/gophercon_africa_2026_2_women.jpg" alt="Two GopherCon Africa 2026 attendees smiling together in front of a backdrop covered in Go logos"/>

Bridge Foundry believes in a future where diversity is normal, and that the
people who make technology should reflect the society it serves. GopherCon
Africa shows that mission at work:

- **Expanding to communities underrepresented in tech.** Since the first
  RailsBridge workshop in 2009, Bridge Foundry has reached further into
  communities with even greater underrepresentation in tech. A conference that
  brings together Go developers from across Africa, alongside guests from
  South America and Europe, is exactly that kind of growth.
- **More women in the room.** Our first workshop changed the makeup of the San
  Francisco Ruby meetup almost overnight. Seeing the number of women at
  GopherCon Africa more than double in a single year echoes that same story.
- **Removing barriers to participation.** Sponsoring 15 attendees from the
  Uganda Go community means cost didn't decide who got to learn and connect.
- **Students become teachers.** Our model depends on today's learners becoming
  tomorrow's teachers, mentors and organizers. With half the attendees early in
  their Go careers and new meetups starting across the continent, GopherCon
  Africa is building that next generation of community leaders.
- **Self-organizing, everywhere.** Bridge Foundry's reach has always come from
  volunteers organizing in their own cities. The spread of new Go meetups
  across African countries is that distributed model in action.
